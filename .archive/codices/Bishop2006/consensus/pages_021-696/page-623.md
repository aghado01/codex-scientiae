12.29 ($\star\star$) Suppose that two variables $z_1$ and $z_2$ are independent so that $p(z_1, z_2) = p(z_1)p(z_2)$. Show that the covariance matrix between these variables is diagonal. This shows that independence is a sufﬁcient condition for two variables to be uncorrelated. Now consider two variables $y_1$ and $y_2$ in which $-1 \le y_1 \le 1$ and $y_2 = y_1^2$. Write down the conditional distribution $p(y_2|y_1)$ and observe that this is dependent on $y_1$, showing that the two variables are not independent. Now show that the covariance matrix between these two variables is again diagonal. To do this, use the relation $p(y_1, y_2) = p(y_1)p(y_2|y_1)$ to show that the off-diagonal terms are zero. This counter-example shows that zero correlation is not a sufﬁcient condition for independence.
