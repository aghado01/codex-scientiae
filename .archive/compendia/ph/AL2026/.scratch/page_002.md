# 1 Introduction

Persistent homology analysis has been invented and well-developed in recent decades, regarded as one of the main tools in topological data analysis (TDA for short) Edelsbrunner et al. ( 2002 ). The standard workflow in the persistent homology analysis is that we first construct a filtration of complexes (or more generally, topological spaces) from the input data, and then record the homological cycles (holes) appearing in the filtration, not only the number but also the time of cycles when they get born and vanish.

In more detail, a filtration of topological spaces can be defined as a (covariant) functor F : P → Top from a poset P as a category to the category of topological spaces Top (morphisms are continuous maps). If we let G be an abelian group and H q ( ; G ): Top → Ab be the q -th homology functor from Top to the category of abelian groups, then H q ( ; G ) ◦ F : P → Ab is called the q -th persistent homology . In most situations, we would let G be a field k and require the tameness on the filtration, meaning that the poset P is finite and each topological space F ( x ) has finite-dimensional q -th homology (see Cohen-Steiner et al. ( 2007 )). Hence the q -th persistent homology H q ( ; k ) ◦ F : P → mod k becomes a functor from the poset as a category to the category mod k of finite-dimensional k -vector spaces.

As a mathematical generalization, it is natural to forget about the process of taking homology and directly consider the (covariant) functor M : P → mod k , known as the definition of persistence module (over P ) in TDA community nowadays. This point of view makes it possible to study persistence modules in the context of representation theory. By convention, we call H ⋆ ( ; k ) ◦ F (resp. M ) the one-parameter persistent homology (resp. 1-dimensional persistence module ) if P is a totally ordered set. In this case, the construction of the filtration depends only on a single parameter. With the study of zigzag persistence , representations of a zigzag partially ordered set ( zigzag poset for short, including totally ordered set as a special case) are proposed and called zigzag modules Carlsson and De Silva ( 2010 ); Botnan and Lesnick ( 2018 ). This type of persistence modules can be also regarded as representations of a Dynkin quiver of type A , and they are uniquely determined by all intervals of underlying ordered sets Crawley-Boevey ( 2015 ); Carlsson and De Silva ( 2010 ); Botnan ( 2017 ), hence we can visualize the persistent homology by drawing the persistence diagram or persistence barcodes, presenting the multiset of intervals Zomorodian and Carlsson ( 2005 ).

Nevertheless, in some applications, the construction of a filtration depends on multiple parameters, causing the underlying poset P to no longer be of Dynkin type A . Hence, it is also necessary to study the persistence module for different types of poset. In the context of multi-parameter persistent homology, for example, the product poset of d posets of Dynkin type A for some d > 1 is commonly considered and called the d D-grid (see Definition 2.12 for the 2D-grid as an example) Dey and Xin ( 2018 ).
