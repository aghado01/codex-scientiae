Interval modules are indecomposable and can be used to describe zigzag modules. This follows from a well-known result about the decomposability of quiver representations since the 70s [ 28 ]. In the context of topological data analysis, zigzag modules were introduced in [ 11 ], together with a constructive proof of such a decomposition.

Theorem 6.6. Every persistence module can be uniquely decomposed, up to isomorphism, as a direct sum of indecomposables. Specifically, for zigzag modules these indecomposables are interval modules.

In particular, given a zigzag module M , there exists a multiset S (i.e. a set where multiple instances of the same element are allowed) of intervals such that M ∼ = I ∈ S I I . The multiset S is denoted as the barcode of M . In addition, string modules can also be seen as the interval modules of specific zigzag modules, as explained in Section 8 .

# 7. Conley-Morse Persistence Barcode

Let TD be a transition diagram for a zigzag filtration of block decompositions B . Note that we have a natural arrangement of index pairs by columns corresponding to the stages of the construction. In particular, Proposition 5.16 tells us that there is a poset underlying every transition diagram, which we denote by P from now on. By applying the homology functor to TD , we obtain a persistence module M over P . It consists of Conley indices and linear maps induced by inclusions. We call it the Conley-Morse persistence module . Before studying its decomposition, we introduce two crucial properties of TD and M .

Lemma 7.1. Let TD be a transition diagram. Consider two index pairs in the same column. If there is an arrow in the induced poset P representing an inclusion ( P p,λ ,E p,λ ) → ( P q,λ ,E q,λ ) then the induced homomorphism in homology is 0.

Proof. By construction the vertical inclusions appear only within an AR-split diagram. The splitting theorem (Theorem 5.12 ) guarantees that the induced linear map equals 0. □

Remark 7.2 . Since all vertical arrows in TD come from the concatenation of two inclusions in an AR-split diagram, there are no vertical arrows in the Hasse diagram of P . In addition, due to the construction of the transition diagram (see Section 5.2 ), every node in the Hasse diagram is the source of at most one arrow and the target of at most one arrow coming from the preceding column.
