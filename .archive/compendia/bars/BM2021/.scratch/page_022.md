The curves given in Fig. 7 illustrate the differences existing between the robust and classical estimates of the additive components. In particular, the classical estimate of the additive component $\eta_1$, related to RM, has a lower peak than the robust one for high values of RM. Besides, the curve takes larger values for the classical fit than for the robust one when RM varies between 4 and 6.5. Similarly, the classical estimate of $\eta_2$ is below the robust one for values smaller than 6.1, while those related to the logarithm of LSTAT are above the robust estimate when the covariate takes values smaller than 2.

The presence of possible atypical data is suspected from the differences arising between the estimations of the additive components and the regression parameter. To identify these atypical observations, we use the residuals obtained from the robust fit. Their boxplot displayed in Fig. 8 identifies as outliers the 21 atypical observations labeled 8, 158, 182, 215, 227, 233, 343, 365, 369, 370, 371, 372, 373, 376, 381, 392, 408, 409, 410, 454 and 506, representing around a 4% of the whole sample. We repeat the analysis using the classical procedure, after removing these potential atypical data. From now on, we indicate with the upper script $(-\mathrm{out})$ the classical estimates obtained without these 21 observations. As with the full data set, the classical BIC selected the same number of knots, i.e., $k = 5$, for all components. The dashed red lines in Fig. 9 correspond to $\hat{\eta}_{j,ls}^{(-\mathrm{out})}$, that is, to the classical fit computed without the potential outliers. For comparison, we also plot, in blue solid lines, the robust estimators already displayed in Fig. 7.
