For   the   estimators   of   the   regression   parameter   β,   we   considered   as   summary   measures   the   bias,   standard   deviation   and   the   mean   square   error   of   each   component,   denoted bias, sd and mse,   respectively.   Tables 1 and   2 report   the   results   for   the   estimators   of   β 1 and   β 2,   respectively.

As expected, for clean data sets, the three estimators of β behave similarly (see Tables 1 and 2 ). When estimating the regression   coeﬃcient   β,   higher   standard   deviations   are   obtained   both   for   the   L 1 and   MM -estimator   due   to   the   loss   of   eﬃciency.   However,   in   most   cases,   the   mean   square   error   of   the   classical   and   MM -methods   are   similar,   since   the   MM estimators   present   smaller   biases.   Note   also   that,   as   in   regression   models,   the   L 1 estimator   is   less   eﬃcient   than   the   MM procedure.   Regarding   the   behavior of   the   estimates   of   β under   contamination,   scenario   C 1 mainly   affects   the   variability   of   the   classical   regression   estimator   (see   Tables 1 and   2 ).   This   performance   is   related   to   the   fact   that   the   errors   are   still   centered   for   this   scheme   but   with   a   larger   dispersion.   The   vertical   asymmetric   outliers   introduced   under   C 2 increase   even   more   the   effect   on   the   variability   of   the   least   squares   procedure   already   observed   under   C 1,   leading   to   mean   square   errors   which   are   more   than   600   times   those   obtained   under   C 0.  Finally,   the   high–leverage   outliers   introduced   under   C 3 have   a   damaging   effect   on   the   classical   estimators   of   β that   become   completely   uninformative,   see   Tables 1 and   2 and   Figs.   S.20   and   S.21   in   the   supplementary   ﬁle,   since   the   absolute   bias   is   enlarged   more   than   800   times   affecting   the   mean   square   error.   A   similar   behavior is   observed   for   the   L 1 estimator,   since   as   mentioned   in   the   introduction   quantiles   estimators   are   related   to   unbounded   loss   functions.   Unlike   the   least   squares   and   L 1 approaches,   the   MM -procedure   is   stable   through   all   considered   contamination   and   models.   The   bias   and   mean   square   error   of   the   estimators   of   β,   reported   in   Tables 1 and   2,   show   that   the   robust   MM -procedure   is   highly   resistant   against   the   contamination   scenarios   considered   here,   even   when   bad   high–leverage   points   are   present   in   the   sample.
