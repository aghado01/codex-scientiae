To have a deeper insight on the performance of $\hat{\eta}_1$ and $\hat{\eta}_2$, Figs. 3 and 4 display the obtained estimated curves in black dashed lines, under Model 3. As it is well-known when using splines, for both classical and robust fits, estimating problems may arise near the boundaries; for that reason, we show here the estimates $\hat{\eta}_1$ and $\hat{\eta}_2$ evaluated on a grid of 900 equispaced points within the interval $[0.05, 0.95]$. In addition, Figs. 5 and 6 give the functional boxplot of $\hat{\eta}_1$ and $\hat{\eta}_2$, also under Model 3. Functional boxplots, introduced by Sun and Genton (2011), are useful to visualize a collection of curves. The area in purple represents the 50% inner band of curves, the solid blue and the dotted red lines correspond to the whiskers and outlying curves, respectively. Moreover, the black line indicates the deepest function. We also plot the true functions in solid green line to facilitate comparisons.

As appreciated in Figs. 3 and 4, under $C_2$, the quantile procedure leads to one curve taking large values, both when estimating $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$. For that reason, in order to capture the global behavior of most estimates, in Figs. 5 and 6 we have considered a reduced vertical axis (between $-1.5$ and $1.5$) for the $L_1$ estimator when considering contamination $C_2$. Analogous plots under Model 6 are given in Figs. S.7 to S.10 in the supplementary file. Besides, Figs. S.11 to S.18 of the supplement display the functional boxplots of the three estimates for the other considered models.

As shown in Table 4, for clean samples, the 5%-mise and the medise of the three estimators exhibit similar results. Moreover, Figs. 3 to 6 show that for clean samples, the least squares, quantile or MM-procedure lead to similar estimates. However, it should be noticed that, when considering the $L_1$ method, the true curve $\eta_1$ approaches to the upper boundary of the 50% inner band for values close to 0.5, explaining the larger bias reported in Table 4 in this case. As expected, when considering the functional boxplots, the box and whiskers for the MM-fits are slightly larger than those obtained by the classical approach due to a loss of efficiency.

In contrast, the damage caused to the least squares estimators by contamination $C_2$ on the ise is clearly illustrated both in Table 4 and in Figs. S.3 to S.6 in the supplementary file. For the classical estimators, the ise of $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$, across all models, are larger than those obtained for both the $L_1$ or MM-counterparts. In particular, the 5%-mise or medise of the classical procedure are more than 350 times those obtained with the MM-method in all models. Contamination $C_1$ also affects the ise of the classical estimators of the additive components, but in a smaller degree since the ratio between the summary measure obtained for the classical and robust MM-estimates is never larger than 10.

It is worth mentioning that contamination $C_3$ distorts the least squares and $L_1$ estimators of $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$, under Model 3, and those of $\eta_1$ under Models 2 and 6, since larger values of the medise are obtained. Furthermore, as reflected in Figs. 5 and 6, the true functions are not contained in the region containing the 50% central curves (see also Figs. S.9 and S.13 in the supplementary file). This bias distortion is also reflected on the squared integrated bias, see Table 5. In particular, when estimating $\eta_1$, under Models 2, 3 and 6, the mean integrated squared bias is more than 1000 times those obtained under $C_0$, for both the least squares and $L_1$ estimators. The worst situation arises under Model 3, where the ratio between mean integrated squared bias of the classical procedure under $C_3$ and under $C_0$ is larger than 20000 for $\hat{\eta}_1$ and almost 8000 when estimating $\eta_2$. A similar conclusion can be obtained for the quantile procedure, showing that in some settings high-leverage points may affect not only the $L_1$ estimates of the regression parameter $\beta$ but also those of the additive components. In contrast, when considering Models 1, 4 and 5, high-leverage outliers have a low impact on the least squares or quantile estimators of $\eta_1$ and $\eta_2$, only increasing their variability but not affecting their bias, which explains the slight increase observed on the 5%-mise and medise.
