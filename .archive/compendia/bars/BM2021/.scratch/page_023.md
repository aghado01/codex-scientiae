## 7. Final Comments

In this paper, we presented a procedure to robustly estimate the regression parameter and the additive components under a partially linear additive model. The method combines B-splines to smoothly estimate the additive components and MM-estimators. The use of B-splines avoids the higher complexity of kernel methods which require backfitting or marginal integration combined with a profile approach; see, for instance, Li (2000) or Ma and Yang (2011) for a discussion on the computational problems arising in partially linear additive model when using kernel methods. In contrast to Ma and Yang (2011), we do not consider an additional step in the procedure using kernel smoothers to obtain estimators of the nonparametric components for which the limiting distribution can be derived. However, our proposal can be extended to provide a robust spline-backfitted kernel smoother combining the ideas in Ma and Yang (2011) to construct pseudo-observations with the robust kernel smoothers defined in Boente and Fraiman (1989). This important issue is beyond the scope of the paper and will be the object of future work.

For the robust B-spline MM-estimators proposed, consistency results and rates of convergence are obtained. Besides, the asymptotic distribution of the linear regression estimator is derived under mild conditions. The numerical results illustrate the stability of the proposed methods under the considered contaminations and models. We exemplify our proposal on the well known Boston housing data set. The analysis shows that the robust MM-estimators automatically discard influential observations leading to reliable estimates.

An important topic to be discussed is the feasibility of the procedure when the number of additive components is large. Even though, in linear regression models, Smucler (2019) derived consistency results for S and MM-regression estimators when the ratio between the number of covariates and the sample size converges to 0, it is well known that the number of subsamples needed to obtain good candidates for the S-estimator increases rapidly with the number of covariates and that the estimator becomes unreliable if only a small number of candidates is used; see Section 5.7.4 in Maronna et al. (2019). Therefore, under a partial additive linear model, when $p$ is large and taking into account that the total number of parameters may be even larger due to the basis dimension, the computation of the scale estimator which uses an S-estimator may be impractical. As described in Maronna et al. (2019), an alternative is to use the outlier detection method introduced in Peña and Yohai (1999) to compute the initial scale estimator. For large values of $p$ and $q$, some penalization as the Ridge, LASSO or SCAD penalty will be needed in order to make the estimators computable. One possibility is to penalize the objective function in (6) by adding a penalization on the regression parameters if $\beta$ is assumed to be sparse and a group LASSO method for selecting the coefficients of the spline approximations, as done in Huang et al. (2010) for additive models. The study of such robust penalized estimators is an interesting and challenging topic that will be the object of future work.
