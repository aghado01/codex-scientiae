For the estimators of the regression parameter $\beta$, we considered as summary measures the bias, standard deviation and the mean square error of each component, denoted $\mathrm{bias}$, $\mathrm{sd}$ and $\mathrm{mse}$, respectively. Tables 1 and 2 report the results for the estimators of $\beta_1$ and $\beta_2$, respectively.

As expected, for clean data sets, the three estimators of $\beta$ behave similarly (see Tables 1 and 2). When estimating the regression coefficient $\beta$, higher standard deviations are obtained both for the $L_1$ and MM-estimator due to the loss of efficiency. However, in most cases, the mean square error of the classical and MM-methods are similar, since the MM estimators present smaller biases. Note also that, as in regression models, the $L_1$ estimator is less efficient than the MM procedure. Regarding the behavior of the estimates of $\beta$ under contamination, scenario $C_1$ mainly affects the variability of the classical regression estimator (see Tables 1 and 2). This performance is related to the fact that the errors are still centered for this scheme but with a larger dispersion. The vertical asymmetric outliers introduced under $C_2$ increase even more the effect on the variability of the least squares procedure already observed under $C_1$, leading to mean square errors which are more than 600 times those obtained under $C_0$. Finally, the high-leverage outliers introduced under $C_3$ have a damaging effect on the classical estimators of $\beta$ that become completely uninformative (see Tables 1 and 2 and Figs. S.20 and S.21 in the supplementary file), since the absolute bias is enlarged more than 800 times, affecting the mean square error. A similar behavior is observed for the $L_1$ estimator, since as mentioned in the introduction quantile estimators are related to unbounded loss functions. Unlike the least squares and $L_1$ approaches, the MM-procedure is stable through all considered contamination and models. The bias and mean square error of the estimators of $\beta$, reported in Tables 1 and 2, show that the robust MM-procedure is highly resistant against the contamination scenarios considered here, even when bad high-leverage points are present in the sample.

Taking into account that in Section 4 the asymptotic distribution of the MM-regression estimators was obtained, and to illustrate the asymptotic performance of the robust estimates, we have computed for each component of $\beta$ the mean over replications of the estimated standard errors, under Model 3. The results for the other models are given in the supplementary file. These estimated standard errors were obtained according to the expression for the asymptotic covariance matrices given in Theorem 4.1. When using the classical estimators, the asymptotic covariance reduces to $\Sigma(\theta, \sigma) = \sigma^2 A^{-1}$. For the least squares estimator, we computed $\hat{\Sigma}$ evaluating $\hat{\sigma}$ as the standard deviation of the residuals obtained from the classical fit and the matrix $A$ as $\hat{A} = (1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\{Z_i - \hat{h}^*(X_i)\}\{Z_i - \hat{h}^*(X_i)\}^t$, where $\hat{h}^*$ minimizes the function $\Upsilon$ defined in (18). When considering the MM-estimators, the estimators of the asymptotic variances were computed as the diagonal elements of $\hat{\Sigma} = \hat{B}^{-1}\hat{D}(\hat{B}^{-1})^t$, following the description to robustly estimate $h^*$ given in Section 4.1. Table 3 reports the estimated standard errors, denoted $\widehat{\mathrm{se}}$, and the coverage probability $\widehat{\mathrm{cov.prob}}$ for a 95% confidence interval, under Model 3, for the least squares and MM-estimators. The coverage probability corresponds to the proportion of times that the asymptotic confidence interval contains the true parameter. To facilitate comparisons, we also present the bias and standard deviation over replications of $\hat{\beta}_j$ already given in Tables 1 and 2. The obtained results reflect that the estimated standard errors are close to the standard deviations obtained over replications, showing that the proposed procedure to estimate the asymptotic covariance matrix is quite accurate.
