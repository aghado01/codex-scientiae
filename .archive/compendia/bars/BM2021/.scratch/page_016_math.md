To   have   a   deeper   insight   on   the   performance   of     η 1 and     η 2,   Figs. 3 and   4 display   the   obtained   estimated   curves   in   black   dashed   lines,   under   Model   3.   As   it   is   well-known   when   using   splines,   for   both   classical   and   robust   ﬁts,   estimating   problems   may   arise   near   the   boundaries,   for   that   reason,   we   show   here   the   estimates     η 1 and     η 2 evaluated   on   a   grid   of   900   equispaced   points   within   the   interval   [ 0.05,   0.95 ].  In   addition,   Figs. 5 and   6 give   the   functional   boxplot   of     η 1 and     η 2,   also   under   Model   3.   Functional   boxplots,   introduced   by   Sun   and   Genton   ( 2011 ),   are   useful   to   visualize   a   collection   of   curves.   The   area   in   purple   represents   the   50%   inner   band   of   curves,   the   solid   blue   and   the   dotted   red   lines   correspond   to   the   whiskers   and   outlying   curves,   respectively.   Moreover,   the   black   line   indicates   the   deepest   function.   We   also   plot   the   true   functions   in   solid   green   line   to   facilitate   comparisons.

As   appreciated   in   Figs. 3 and   4,   under   C 2,   the   quantile   procedure   leads   to   one   curve   taking   large   values,   both   when   estimating   η 1 and   η 2.  For   that   reason,   in   order   to   capture   the   global   behavior of   most   estimates,   in   Figs. 5 and   6 we   have   considered   a   reduced   vertical   axis   (between   −   1.5 and   1.5)   for   the   L 1 estimator   when   considering   contamination   C 2.  Analogous   plots   under   Model   6   are   given   in   Figs.   S.7   to   S.10   in   the   supplementary   ﬁle.   Besides,   Figs.   S.11   to   S.18   of   the   supplement   display   the   functional   boxplots   of   the   three   estimates   for   the   other   considered   models.

As   shown   in   Table 4,   for   clean   samples,   the 5%-mise and   the medise of   the   three   estimators   exhibit   similar   results.   Moreover,   Figs. 3 to   6 show   that   for   clean   samples,   the   least   squares,   quantile   or   MM -procedure   lead   to   similar   estimates.   However,   it   should   be   noticed   that,   when   considering   the   L 1 method,   the   true   curve   η 1 approaches   to   the   upper   boundary   of   the   50%   inner   band   for   values   close   to   0.5,   explaining   the   larger   bias   reported   in   Table 4 in   this   case.   As   expected,   when   considering   the   functional   boxplots,   the   box   and   whiskers   for   the   MM -ﬁts   are   slightly   larger   than   those   obtained   by   the   classical   approach   due   to   a   loss   of   eﬃciency.

In   contrast,   the   damage   caused   to   the   least   squares   estimators   by   contamination   C 2 on   the ise is   clearly   illustrated   both   in   Table 4 and   in   Figs.   S.3   to   S.6   in   the   supplementary   ﬁle.   For   the   classical   estimators,   the ise of   η 1 and   η 2,   across   all   models,   are   larger   than   those   obtained   for   both   the   L 1 or   MM -counterparts.   In   particular,   the 5%-mise or medise of   the   classical   procedure   are   more   than   350   times   those   obtained   with   the   MM -method   in   all   models.   Contamination   C 1 also   affects   the ise of   the   classical   estimators   of   the   additive   components,   but   in   a   smaller   degree   since   the   ratio   between   the   summary   measure   obtained   for   the   classical   and   robust   MM -estimates   is   never   larger   than   10.

It is worth mentioning that contamination C 3 distorts the least squares and L 1 estimators of η 1 and η 2, under Model 3 and those of η 1 under Models 2 and 6, since larger values of the medise are obtained. Furthermore, as reflected in Figs. 5 and 6, the true functions are not contained in the region containing the 50% central curves (see also Figs. S.9 and S.13 in the supplementary fi le). This bias distortion is also reflected on the squared integrated bias, see Table 5. In particular, when estimating η 1, under Models 2, 3 and 6, the mean integrated squared bias is more than 1000 times those obtained under C 0, for both the least squares and L 1 estimators. The worst situation arises under Model 3, there the ratio between mean integrated squared bias of the classical procedure under C 3 and under C 0 is larger than 20000 for ̂ η 1 and almost 8000 when estimating η 2.A similar conclusion can be obtained for the quantile procedure, showing that in some settings high-leverage points may affect not only the L 1 estimates of the regression parameter β but also those of the additive components. In contrast, when considering the Models 1, 4 and 5, high-leverage outliers have a low impact on the least squares or quantile estimators of η 1 and η 2 only increasing their variability but not affecting their bias which explains the slight increase observed on the 5%-mise and medise (see, for instance, Figs. S.11, S.12 and S.15 to S.18).
