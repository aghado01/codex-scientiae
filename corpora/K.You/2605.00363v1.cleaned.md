# Abstract

We study likelihood-based inference for the anisotropic hyperbolic wrapped normal distribution on standard hyperbolic space. The model has a manifold-valued location parameter and a full positive definite covariance matrix in tangent coordinates. For independent observations from this family, we analyze the profile maximum likelihood estimator obtained by optimizing the likelihood over the location after profiling out the covariance. To guarantee finite-sample existence, we formulate the estimator on a covariance shell that bounds eigenvalues away from zero and infinity. We prove that this constrained likelihood is well posed, that the anisotropic wrapped normal family is identifiable, and that the estimator is strongly consistent when the true covariance lies in the interior of the shell. In global normal coordinates for the location and log-covariance coordinates for the nuisance parameter, we establish joint asymptotic normality and derive efficient profile inference for the location parameter through the Schur-complement information. We further prove local asymptotic normality of the experiment and obtain the H´jek–Le Cam local asymptotic minimax lower bound under squared geodesic loss. The profile estimator attains this bound for truncated squared loss, and for ordinary squared loss under a uniform-integrability condition. We also give an explicit computational form of the estimator based on spectral clipping of the empirical tangent covariance, and present a Monte Carlo calibration study showing that the finite-sample scaled location risk and Wald coverage agree with the asymptotic theory.

# 1 Introduction

Hyperbolic geometry has become a standard ambient geometry for data with latent tree-like or hierarchical structure, both in geometric statistics and representation learning (Nickel and Kiela; 2017, 2018). A central probabilistic construction in that literature is the hyperbolic wrapped normal (HWN) distribution introduced by Nagano et al. (2019). Their model is defined by sampling a Gaussian vector in the tangent space at the hyperbolic origin, parallel-transporting it to a desired base point, and projecting it onto the manifold. Despite the importance of the model, inferential theory for its parameters appears to be largely absent from the statistical literature. The gap is especially visible in the anisotropic case, where the covariance parameter is a full positive definite matrix. The anisotropic model is substantially richer than the isotropic one (Said et al.; 2018; You; 2026), but also more delicate. That is, the location parameter lives on a noncompact manifold, the likelihood includes a curvature-induced Jacobian term, and the unrestricted conditional covariance maximization can fail to have a finite maximizer when the empirical tangent covariance becomes singular.

This paper develops a rigorous likelihood-based theory for the anisotropic hyperbolic wrapped normal family on the standard hyperbolic space H d , where the estimator is developed by the profile likelihood framework (Box and Cox; 1964; Cox; 1970). The location parameter is estimated by maximizing the likelihood after profiling out the covariance in tangent coordinates. The correct minimax conclusion for this route is not exact finite-sample minimaxity on the whole manifold. Rather, it is local asymptotic minimaxity in the sense of H´jek and Le Cam. This is the natural decision-theoretic endpoint of regular likelihood theory on curved noncompact parameter spaces (Hajek; 1972; van der Vaart; 1998). Our contributions are as follows.

1. We formulate a shell-constrained anisotropic likelihood on

$$
\Theta = \mathbb { H } ^ { d } \times \mathcal { K } , \quad \mathcal { K } = \{ \Sigma \in \mathcal { S } ^ { + + } ( d ) \colon \lambda _ { - } I \preceq \Sigma \preceq \lambda _ { + } I \} ,
$$

with 0 < λ − < λ + fixed and the true covariance in the interior. This resolves the finite-sample non-attainment issue for the unrestricted covariance likelihood while preserving the practical closed form of the covariance profile whenever the empirical tangent covariance lies in the shell.

- 2. We prove identifiability of the anisotropic HWN family and existence of the shell-constrained joint and profile MLEs.
- 3. We prove strong consistency by combining a coercivity argument in the location parameter, a compact-uniform law of large numbers, and Kullback–Leibler identification in a global normal coordinate chart.
- 4. In local Euclidean coordinates ( α,β ), where α = Log µ 0 ( µ ) and β = vech(log Σ), we prove joint asymptotic normality of the MLE and efficient asymptotic normality of the profiled location estimator. The efficient covariance for location is the inverse Schur complement of the nuisance block of the Fisher information.
- 5. We establish local asymptotic normality (LAN) of the anisotropic HWN experiment and deduce the H´jek–Le Cam local asymptotic minimax lower bound. The profile MLE attains

the corresponding bound for bounded and truncated squared geodesic losses; the untruncated squared-loss conclusion is obtained under an explicit local uniform-integrability condition.

6. We translate the estimator into a concrete computational recipe. In particular, we derive the closed spectral form of the shell-constrained covariance profile, explain how the outer optimization can be performed in global normal coordinates, and include a Monte Carlo study assessing finite-sample calibration of the asymptotic theory.

Related literature. General asymptotic theory for M-estimators and maximum likelihood estimators follows the classical lines of Wald (1949), Hoadley (1971), and van der Vaart (1998). For local asymptotic minimax theory we rely on Hajek (1972) and the modern presentation in van der Vaart (1998). Background on the geometry of Hadamard manifolds and normal coordinates can be found in Petersen (2006), and the matrix-logarithm chart for positive definite matrices is standard (Bhatia; 2009). For optimization on manifolds we will use the general framework of Absil et al. (2008). We emphasize that our problem is different from intrinsic Frechet mean estimation on manifolds, treated for example by Bhattacharya and Patrangenaru (2003). Here the target is a fully parametric anisotropic wrapped family rather than a nonparametric notion of center. We also note that our LAN and local minimax arguments fit into the general asymptotic-statistical paradigm developed by Le Cam and Lo Yang (2000), although the concrete verification here is carried out directly for the anisotropic HWN model.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the anisotropic HWN model, its density, the sample likelihood, and the shell-constrained profile estimator. Section 3 develops the analytic preliminaries needed for likelihood theory, including moment bounds, derivative envelopes, score identities, and a compact-uniform law of large numbers. Section 4 proves existence, identifiability, strong consistency, and asymptotic inactivity of the covariance shell. Section 5 establishes joint asymptotic normality and efficient profile inference for the location parameter. Section 6 proves local asymptotic normality and derives the local asymptotic minimax consequences under squared geodesic loss. Section 7 describes the computational form of the estimator, including the spectral covariance profile and the global coordinate used for location optimization. Section 8 presents a Monte Carlo calibration study. The proofs are collected in the Appendix.

# 2.3 Likelihood, covariance profiling, and the shell-constrained estimator

Fix observations X 1 ,...,X n ∈ H d . The sample log-likelihood is

$$
\ell _ { n } ( \mu , \Sigma ) = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } m _ { \mu , \Sigma } ( X _ { i } ) .
$$

For a fixed µ , define the empirical tangent covariance

$$
S _ { n } ( \mu ) \coloneqq \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) ^ { \top } .
$$

Then (6) can be rewritten as

$$
\ell _ { n } ( \mu , \Sigma ) = - \frac { n } { 2 } \log \det \Sigma - \frac { n } { 2 } \ t r ( S _ { n } ( \mu ) \Sigma ^ { - 1 } ) - ( d - 1 ) \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \varphi ( T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) ) .
$$

If S n ( µ ) is positive definite and the covariance parameter is unrestricted over S ++ ( d ), the conditional maximizer is Σ = S n ( µ ), exactly as in the Euclidean Gaussian model. However, if S n ( µ ) is singular, no finite unrestricted maximizer exists and the likelihood can be increased indefinitely by shrinking the covariance in directions orthogonal to the sample span. To avoid this pathology, fix constants 0 < λ − < λ + and define the compact shell

$$
\mathcal { K } \coloneqq \{ \Sigma \in \mathcal { S } ^ { + + } ( d ) \colon \lambda _ { - } I \preceq \Sigma \preceq \lambda _ { + } I \} .
$$

We assume throughout that the true covariance Σ 0 lies in int( K ).

For each µ , define the shell-constrained covariance profile

$$
\widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \mu ) \in \arg \min _ { \Sigma \in \mathcal { K } } \left \{ \log \det \Sigma + \text {tr} \left ( S _ { n } ( \mu ) \Sigma ^ { - 1 } \right ) \right \} .
$$

Then any shell-constrained profile MLE is a measurable maximizer of

$$
\hat { \mu } _ { n } \in \arg \max _ { \mu \in \mathbb { H } ^ { d } } \ell _ { n } \left ( \mu , \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \mu ) \right ) , \quad \hat { \Sigma } _ { n } \colon = \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) .
$$

The next proposition constructs the basic covariance-profile algebra.

Proposition 2.2 (Conditional covariance optimization) . For every symmetric positive semidefinite matrix S , the map

$$
\Sigma \mapsto f _ { S } ( \Sigma ) \colon = \log \det \Sigma + t r ( S \Sigma ^ { - 1 } )
$$

is continuous on K and hence attains its minimum on K . If S ∈ S ++ ( d ) , then the unique unconstrained minimizer on S ++ ( d ) is S . Consequently, if S ∈ int( K ) , then the unique constrained minimizer on K is also S .

In the practically important event S n ( µ ) ∈ int( K ), the profile objective reduces to

$$
\mu \mapsto - \frac { n } { 2 } \log \det S _ { n } ( \mu ) - ( d - 1 ) \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \varphi ( T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) ) ,
$$

up to the additive constant − nd/ 2. This is the objective one would optimize in practice when the empirical tangent covariance is positive definite.

Lemma 3.1 (Moment and exponential tail bounds) . Let X ∼ HWN d ( µ 0 , Σ 0 ) . Then:

- (a) for every q ≥ 1 , E [ ρ ( o,X ) q ] < ∞ ;

# 4 Existence, identifiability, and strong consistency

We now establish the basic structural properties of the likelihood framework. In particular, we show that the shell-constrained likelihood admits a maximizer, that the anisotropic HWN family is identifiable, and that the resulting estimator is strongly consistent under standard sampling assumptions. These results provide the foundation for the subsequent asymptotic analysis.

Theorem 4.1 (Existence of the shell-constrained MLE) . For every sample ( X 1 ,...,X n ) ∈ ( H d ) n , the shell-constrained joint likelihood

$$
( \mu , \Sigma ) \mapsto \ell _ { n } ( \mu , \Sigma )
$$

admits at least one maximizer on Θ = H d × K . Consequently, the shell-constrained profile MLE (11) exists.

The existence result ensures that the constrained optimization problem is well posed for every sample. We next verify that the underlying statistical model is identifiable, which guarantees that the population likelihood has a unique maximizer.

Theorem 4.2 (Identifiability) . The anisotropic HWN family { HWN d ( µ, Σ) : ( µ, Σ) ∈ H d ×S ++ ( d ) } is identifiable. That is, if HWN d ( µ, Σ) = HWN d ( µ ′ , Σ ′ ) , then µ = µ ′ and Σ = Σ ′ .

To study asymptotic properties, we impose standard regularity conditions. The first ensures that the true parameter lies in the interior of the constraint set, so that the shell does not bind asymptotically. The second is the usual nondegeneracy condition on the Fisher information.

The interior condition is required only because the shell is a technical device. The shell itself may be chosen arbitrarily large provided it contains the true covariance in its interior. The positive-definiteness of the information matrix is the standard nondegeneracy condition needed for asymptotic normality and LAN.

We now turn to consistency of the estimator. The proof follows a classical likelihood argument based on Kullback–Leibler identification, localization of the maximizer on a compact set, and a compact-uniform law of large numbers.

Theorem 4.4 (Strong consistency) . Assume that X 1 ,X 2 ,... are i.i.d. from HWN d ( µ 0 , Σ 0 ) with ( µ 0 , Σ 0 ) ∈ H d × int( K ) . Let (ˆ µ n , ˆ Σ n ) be any measurable shell-constrained maximizer of the likelihood. Then

$$
\hat { \mu } _ { n } \rightarrow \mu _ { 0 } , \quad \hat { \Sigma } _ { n } \rightarrow \Sigma _ { 0 } \quad a l m o s t \ s u r e l y .
$$

Equivalently, in the Euclidean chart,

$$
\hat { \vartheta } _ { n } \rightarrow \vartheta _ { 0 } \quad a . s .
$$

The next proposition shows that the shell constraint becomes inactive asymptotically at the estimator. This provides the link between the constrained formulation used for existence and the unconstrained profile estimator employed in practice.

Proposition 4.5 (Asymptotic inactivity of the shell) . Under the assumptions of Theorem 4.4,

$$
S _ { n } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) \rightarrow \Sigma _ { 0 } \quad a . s .
$$

Consequently,

$$
\mathbb { P } ( S _ { n } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) \in \text {int} ( \mathcal { K } ) ) \to 1 ,

$$
\hat { \Sigma } _ { n } = S _ { n } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) .

# 5 Asymptotic normality and efficient profile inference

We now develop the second-order asymptotic theory for the shell-constrained maximum likelihood estimator. Building on the consistency results of the previous section, we derive an expansion for the estimator, establish joint asymptotic normality, and characterize the efficient limit distribution of the profiled location parameter. All statements are made in the Euclidean chart ϑ = ( α, β ) introduced in (13)-(14). Throughout this section, write

$$
s _ { \vartheta } ( x ) = \partial _ { \vartheta } m _ { \vartheta } ( x ) , \quad \dot { s } _ { \vartheta } ( x ) = \partial _ { \vartheta } ^ { 2 } m _ { \vartheta } ( x ) .

We begin with a standard likelihood expansion showing that the estimator admits an asymptotically linear representation. This yields joint asymptotic normality for the full parameter.

Theorem 5.1 (Joint asymptotic normality of the constrained MLE) . Assume that X 1 ,X 2 ,... are i.i.d. from HWN d ( µ 0 , Σ 0 ) and that Assumption 4.3 holds. Then

$$
\sqrt { n } \left ( \hat { \vartheta } _ { n } - \vartheta _ { 0 } \right ) = \mathcal { I } ( \vartheta _ { 0 } ) ^ { - 1 } \frac { 1 } { \sqrt { n } } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } s _ { \vartheta _ { 0 } } ( X _ { i } ) + o _ { p } ( 1 ) ,
$$

and hence

$$
\sqrt { n } \left ( \hat { \vartheta } _ { n } - \vartheta _ { 0 } \right ) \, \Rightarrow \, N \left ( 0 , \mathcal { I } ( \vartheta _ { 0 } ) ^ { - 1 } \right ) .
$$

To study the effect of the covariance parameter as a nuisance, we decompose the Fisher information matrix according to the partition ϑ = ( α,β ):

$$
\mathcal { I } ( \vartheta _ { 0 } ) = \begin{pmatrix} \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \alpha } & \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \beta } \\ \mathcal { I } _ { \beta \alpha } & \mathcal { I } _ { \beta \beta } \end{pmatrix} .

$$

and This shows that profiling out the covariance parameter leads to an asymptotically efficient estimator for the location parameter in the presence of nuisance covariance.

$$
\sqrt { n } \, \hat { \alpha } _ { n } \Rightarrow N ( 0 , \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \cdot \beta } ^ { - 1 } ) , \quad \hat { \alpha } _ { n } \coloneqq \log _ { \mu _ { 0 } } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) \in \mathbb { R } ^ { d } .

# 6 Local asymptotic normality and local asymptotic minimaxity

We next turn from large-sample distribution theory to the local decision-theoretic implications of the likelihood expansion. The goal is to show that, after localizing the anisotropic HWN experiment around the true parameter, the model converges to a Gaussian shift experiment with Fisher information. This LAN structure then yields the local asymptotic minimax lower bound for estimating the location parameter under squared geodesic loss.

# 6.1 The LAN expansion

For h ∈ R p , define the local parameter perturbation

$$
\vartheta _ { n , h } \colon = \vartheta _ { 0 } + \frac { h } { \sqrt { n } } .
$$

Because Σ 0 lies in the interior of the covariance shell, for every compact C ⊂ R p the covariance component of ϑ n,h also belongs to the interior of the shell for all sufficiently large n , uniformly over h ∈ C . Define the log-likelihood ratio

$$
\Lambda _ { n } ( h ) \colon = \log \frac { d P _ { \vartheta _ { n , h } } ^ { n } } { d P _ { \vartheta _ { 0 } } ^ { n } } = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \left ( m _ { \vartheta _ { n , h } } ( X _ { i } ) - m _ { \vartheta _ { 0 } } ( X _ { i } ) \right ) .

$$
\Delta _ { n } \colon = \frac { 1 } { \sqrt { n } } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } s _ { \vartheta _ { 0 } } ( X _ { i } ) .
$$

The following result is the key bridge between the likelihood expansion of the previous section and the minimax analysis below.

$$

$$

# 6.2 Local geodesic loss and Euclidean normal coordinates

Because the parameter of interest lives on the manifold H d , the relevant loss is squared geodesic loss:

$$
L ( \hat { \mu } , \mu ) \colon = \rho ( \hat { \mu } , \mu ) ^ { 2 } .
$$

To connect this loss to LAN theory, we need the local equivalence between geodesic distance and Euclidean distance in normal coordinates.

Lemma 6.2 (Local equivalence of squared geodesic and Euclidean loss) . Let u n ,v n ∈ T µ 0 H d satisfy √ nu n and √ nv n bounded. Then

$$
n \, \rho ( \exp _ { \mu _ { 0 } } ( u _ { n } ) , \exp _ { \mu _ { 0 } } ( v _ { n } ) ) ^ { 2 } - \left \| \sqrt { n } \left ( u _ { n } - v _ { n } \right ) \right \| ^ { 2 } \to 0 .
$$

More generally, the convergence is uniform whenever ( √ nu n , √ nv n ) ranges over a fixed compact subset of T µ 0 H d × T µ 0 H d .

With this local loss equivalence in hand, we can translate the Gaussian-shift lower bound back to the original manifold-valued estimation problem.

# 6.3 The H´ajek–Le Cam lower bound

Write h = ( h α ,h β ) according to the location/nuisance decomposition. Define the local alternatives

$$
\mu _ { n , h } \coloneqq \exp _ { \mu _ { 0 } } ( h _ { \alpha } / \sqrt { n } ) , \quad \Sigma _ { n , h } \coloneqq \exp ( \text {mat} ( \beta _ { 0 } + h _ { \beta } / \sqrt { n } ) ) , \quad P _ { n , h } \coloneqq P _ { ( \mu _ { n , h } , \Sigma _ { n , h } ) } ^ { n } .
$$

For an estimator ˜ µ n , write

$$
\tilde { a } _ { n } \colon = \sqrt { n } \, \log _ { \mu _ { 0 } } ( \tilde { \mu } _ { n } ) \in \mathbb { R } ^ { d } .
$$

This local coordinate is globally defined because H d is Hadamard.

The proof of the lower bound uses two facts. First, the LAN experiment with nuisance covariance has efficient information for the location coordinate equal to

$$
\mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \alpha \cdot \beta } = \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \alpha } - \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \beta } \mathcal { I } _ { \beta \beta } ^ { - 1 } \mathcal { I } _ { \beta \alpha } .
$$

Second, in a Hadamard manifold with nonpositive sectional curvature the exponential map is

$$
\rho ( \text {Exp} _ { \mu _ { 0 } } \, u , \text {Exp} _ { \mu _ { 0 } } \, v ) \geq \| u - v \| .
$$

This follows from the Cartan–Hadamard theorem and the Rauch comparison theorem (do Carmo; 1992). In hyperbolic space it can also be checked directly from the hyperbolic law of cosines.

The lower bound is stated first for truncated squared geodesic loss, which avoids unnecessary integrability assumptions on arbitrary estimator sequences. The ordinary squared-loss bound then follows by monotone convergence.

Theorem 6.3 (Local asymptotic minimax lower bound) . Assume that the local experiments are generated by the anisotropic HWN family and that Assumption 4.3 holds. Let ˜ µ n be any sequence of estimators with values in H d . If Z ∼ N (0 , I − 1 αα · β ) , then for every A < ∞ ,

$$
\lim _ { c \to \infty } \liminf _ { n \to \infty } \sup _ { \| h \| \leq c } \mathbb { E } _ { n , h } \left [ A \wedge n \rho \left ( \tilde { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } \right ) ^ { 2 } \right ] \geq \mathbb { E } [ A \wedge \| Z \| ^ { 2 } ] .
$$

Consequently, tion 4.3 holds. Let Z ∼ N (0 , I -1 αα · β ) . Then, for every fixed A < ∞ and c < ∞ ,

$$
\lim _ { c \to \infty } \liminf _ { n \to \infty } \sup _ { \| h \| \leq c } \mathbb { E } _ { n , h } \left [ n \rho ( \tilde { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } \right ] \geq \text {tr} ( \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \alpha \cdot \beta } ^ { - 1 } ) .
$$

The preceding theorem applies to every estimator and gives the benchmark risk. We now show that the profiled location MLE attains this benchmark. For bounded and truncated losses this follows from regular efficiency. For unbounded squared loss, a uniform-integrability condition is needed to pass from convergence in distribution to convergence of risks.

# 6.4 Attainment by the profile MLE

The lower bound above is unconditional. Attainment by the profile MLE is immediate for bounded and truncated losses from regular efficiency. For ordinary unbounded squared loss, one additional moment condition is needed; without it, convergence in distribution does not imply convergence of risks.

Assumption 6.4 (Local uniform integrability of squared location risk) . For every c < ∞ , the random variables

$$
\{ n \rho ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } \colon n \geq 1 , \ \| h \| \leq c \}
$$

are uniformly integrable under the corresponding laws P n,h . Equivalently,

$$
\lim _ { A \to \infty } \lim _ { n \to \infty } \sup _ { \| h \| \leq c } \mathbb { E } _ { n , h } \left [ n \rho ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } \mathbf 1 \{ n \rho ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } > A \} \right ] = 0 .
$$

Theorem 6.5 (Local asymptotic minimaxity of the profiled location estimator) . Assume that X 1 ,X 2 ,... are i.i.d. from the local anisotropic HWN experiments defined above and that Assump-

$$
\lim _ { n \to \infty } \sup _ { \| h \| \leq c } \left | \mathbb { E } _ { n , h } \left [ A \wedge n \rho ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } \right ] - \mathbb { E } [ A \wedge \| Z \| ^ { 2 } ] \right | = 0 .
$$

Consequently, the profile MLE attains the local asymptotic minimax bound for every truncated squared geodesic loss. Additionally, if Assumption 6.4 holds, then it also attains the untruncated squared-loss bound:

$$
\lim _ { c \to \infty } \lim _ { n \to \infty } \sup _ { \| h \| \leq c } \mathbb { E } _ { n , h } \left [ n \rho ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } , \mu _ { n , h } ) ^ { 2 } \right ] \leq \text {tr} ( \mathcal { I } _ { \alpha \alpha \cdot \beta } ^ { - 1 } ) .
$$

Together with (26) , this yields local asymptotic minimaxity under ordinary squared geodesic loss whenever the stated uniform-integrability condition is satisfied.

The minimax statements proved above are local asymptotic statements, not exact finite-sample minimaxity on the whole manifold. The latter is much more delicate on noncompact curved parameter spaces and does not follow from symmetry alone. For the profile-MLE route, the LAN result of Theorem 6.1 and the H´jek–Le Cam theorem provide the correct and standard decisiontheoretic conclusion. The untruncated squared-loss attainment is deliberately separated from the truncated-loss result because it requires uniform integrability of local risks.

Thus the profile likelihood estimator has the expected optimality property for regular finitedimensional likelihood problems. It is efficient in the LAN limit experiment and locally asymptotically minimax for the location parameter, with the covariance matrix treated as nuisance. The qualification concerning unbounded squared loss is purely technical and reflects the standard distinction between weak convergence and convergence of second moments.

# 7 Computation

The preceding sections established the statistical properties of the shell-constrained profile likelihood estimator. We now describe how the estimator is computed in practice. The main point is that the covariance parameter can be profiled out exactly for each candidate location, leaving a finitedimensional optimization problem only in the location variable. The shell constraint, introduced for finite-sample existence and stability, is handled by a simple spectral clipping operation.

# 7.1 Closed-form covariance profiling

For a fixed location µ , recall the empirical tangent scatter matrix

$$
S _ { n } ( \mu ) \colon = \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) ^ { \top } .
$$

The covariance part of the negative profile log-likelihood is

$$
\Sigma \mapsto \log \det \Sigma + \text {tr} \{ S _ { n } ( \mu ) \Sigma ^ { - 1 } \} , \quad \Sigma \in \mathcal { K } .
$$

Thus the nuisance optimization is a constrained covariance-fitting problem over the spectral shell. The next proposition gives its exact solution.

Proposition 7.1 (Spectral form of the shell-constrained covariance profile) . Let S ∈ S ( d ) be positive semidefinite and write its spectral decomposition as

$$
S = U \ d i a g ( s _ { 1 } , \dots , s _ { d } ) \, U ^ { \top } , \quad s _ { j } \geq 0 .
$$

Then the unique minimizer of

$$
\Sigma \mapsto f _ { S } ( \Sigma ) = \log \det \Sigma + \text {tr} ( S \Sigma ^ { - 1 } ) \quad o v e r \quad \mathcal { K } = \{ \lambda _ { - } I \preceq \Sigma \preceq \lambda _ { + } I \}
$$

is

$$
\widetilde { \Sigma } ( S ) = U \, \text {diag} ( \widetilde { s } _ { 1 } , \dots , \widetilde { s } _ { d } ) \, U ^ { \top } , \quad \widetilde { s } _ { j } = \min \{ \lambda _ { + } , \max \{ \lambda _ { - } , s _ { j } \} \} .
$$

Equivalently, Σ( S ) = g ( S ) by functional calculus, where

$$
g ( s ) = \min \{ \lambda _ { + } , \max \{ \lambda _ { - } , s \} \} .
$$

Following the above, the covariance profile obtained by clipping the eigenvalues of the empirical tangent covariance matrix. Thus each outer location iterate requires only the evaluation of tangent coordinates and one eigendecomposition of a d × d symmetric matrix.

# 7.2 The profiled objective

Combining the likelihood with Proposition 7.1, define

$$
\Sigma _ { n } ( \mu ) \colon = \Sigma \{ S _ { n } ( \mu ) \} .
$$

Up to additive constants independent of µ , the shell-constrained profiled negative log-likelihood is

$$
Q _ { n } ^ { \mathcal { K } } ( \mu ) = \frac { n } { 2 } \log \det \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \mu ) + \frac { n } { 2 } \ t r \{ S _ { n } ( \mu ) \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \mu ) ^ { - 1 } \} + ( d - 1 ) \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \varphi \{ T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) \} .
$$

The shell-constrained profile estimator is any minimizer of

$$
\mu \mapsto Q _ { n } ^ { \mathcal { K } } ( \mu ) ,
$$

and the covariance estimate is then

$$
\hat { \Sigma } _ { n } = \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \hat { \mu } _ { n } ) .
$$

When S n ( µ ) is positive definite and lies in the interior of the shell, the clipping operation is inactive and Σ n ( µ ) = S n ( µ ). In that case,

$$
\ t r \{ S _ { n } ( \mu ) \widetilde { \Sigma } _ { n } ( \mu ) ^ { - 1 } \} = d ,
$$

so the location-dependent part of the criterion reduces to

$$
Q _ { n } ( \mu ) = \frac { n } { 2 } \log \det S _ { n } ( \mu ) + ( d - 1 ) \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \varphi \{ T _ { \mu } ( X _ { i } ) \} .
$$

This is the practical profile objective used in unconstrained computations. By Proposition 4.5, the shell-constrained and practical profile estimators coincide with probability tending to one under the correctly specified model whenever Σ 0 lies in the interior of the shell.

# 7.3 Global coordinates for location optimization

For computation, it is convenient to parameterize the location by a global normal coordinate at the origin:

$$
\nu = \log _ { o } ( \mu ) \in T _ { o } \mathbb { H } ^ { d } \cong \mathbb { R } ^ { d } , \quad \mu ( \nu ) = \exp _ { o } ( \nu ) .
$$

Since H d is Hadamard, the map ν  → µ ( ν ) is a global diffeomorphism. Therefore the outer optimization may be carried out over the unconstrained Euclidean variable ν ∈ R d .

In these coordinates, the shell-constrained objective becomes

$$
\nu \mapsto Q _ { n } ^ { \mathcal { K } } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} ,
$$

where the covariance profile is computed by applying Proposition 7.1 to

$$
S _ { n } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} = \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } T _ { \mu ( \nu ) } ( X _ { i } ) T _ { \mu ( \nu ) } ( X _ { i } ) ^ { \top } .
$$

# 7.4 Optimization and numerical safeguards

The Lorentz-model formulas for Exp µ , Log µ , and parallel transport are explicit (Nagano et al.; 2019). Hence each evaluation of the profiled criterion consists of the following steps:

- (i) map the current Euclidean coordinate ν to µ ( ν ) = Exp o ( ν );
- (ii) compute tangent coordinates T µ ( ν ) ( X i ) for all observations;
- (iii) form the empirical scatter matrix S n { µ ( ν ) } ;

- (iv) compute the profiled covariance Σ n { µ ( ν ) } by spectral clipping;
- (v) evaluate the profiled objective (30).

When the practical objective (31) is used, its derivative formally satisfies

$$
D _ { \nu } Q _ { n } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} = \frac { n } { 2 } \ t r [ S _ { n } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} ^ { - 1 } D _ { \nu } S _ { n } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} ] + ( d - 1 ) \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } D _ { \nu } \varphi \{ T _ { \mu ( \nu ) } ( X _ { i } ) \} ,
$$

where

$$
D _ { \nu } S _ { n } \{ \mu ( \nu ) \} = \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } D _ { \nu } \left [ T _ { \mu ( \nu ) } ( X _ { i } ) T _ { \mu ( \nu ) } ( X _ { i } ) ^ { \top } \right ] .
$$

Hence, one may opt for standard algorithms including quasi-Newton, trust-region, or Riemanniangradient methods (Absil et al.; 2008). In our implementation, the optimization is performed in the global Euclidean coordinate ν , with multiple initializations used in the more anisotropic scenarios.

Several numerical safeguards are important. First, the function

$$
r \mapsto \log \left ( \frac { \sinh r } { r } \right )
$$

should not be evaluated naively at very small or very large r . For small r , we use the expansion

$$
\log \left ( \frac { \sinh r } { r } \right ) = \frac { r ^ { 2 } } { 6 } - \frac { r ^ { 4 } } { 1 8 0 } + O ( r ^ { 6 } ) , \quad r \downarrow 0 .
$$

For large r , we use the stable identity

$$
\log \left ( \frac { \sinh r } { r } \right ) = r - \log ( 2 r ) + \log ( 1 - e ^ { - 2 r } ) .
$$

Second, log-determinants and quadratic forms are computed using eigendecompositions or Cholesky factors rather than explicit matrix inverses. Third, the shell profile is evaluated through eigenvalue clipping, which prevents singular covariance estimates in small samples or nearly degenerate configurations.

A natural initialization is the two-step intrinsic estimator to first compute a sample Fre´chet mean ˆ µ F and then set n

$$
\hat { \Sigma } _ { F } = \frac { 1 } { n } \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } T _ { \hat { \mu } _ { F } } ( X _ { i } ) T _ { \hat { \mu } _ { F } } ( X _ { i } ) ^ { \top } .
$$

The profile likelihood optimization is then initialized at ˆ µ F , with optional random perturbations in the global coordinate ν for multi-start checks.

# 9 Conclusion

This paper develops a likelihood-based inferential theory for the anisotropic hyperbolic wrapped normal distribution on standard hyperbolic space. The model combines a manifold-valued location parameter with a full tangent-space covariance matrix, and therefore requires both geometric and multivariate likelihood arguments. The estimator studied here is the profile maximum likelihood estimator obtained by optimizing over the location after profiling out the covariance parameter.

A technical difficulty in the anisotropic model is that the unrestricted covariance profile may fail to attain a finite maximum when the empirical tangent covariance is singular. We address this by introducing a compact covariance shell whose eigenvalue bounds keep the likelihood well posed. This device guarantees finite-sample existence of the constrained estimator, while remaining asymptotically inactive when the true covariance lies in the interior of the shell. Thus the constrained formulation provides a rigorous foundation for the practical profile likelihood computation.

The main theoretical results establish identifiability, existence of the shell-constrained joint and profile maximum likelihood estimators, strong consistency, joint asymptotic normality, and efficient profile asymptotic normality for the location parameter. The covariance parameter is treated as nuisance for location inference, and the efficient asymptotic covariance is given by the inverse Schur complement of the nuisance information block.
