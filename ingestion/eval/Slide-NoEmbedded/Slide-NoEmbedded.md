# Grading Criteria

| Grading Criteria | Description |
|------------------|-------------|
| Content quality | How complete, accurate, and useful the content is.<br><br>Look for:<br>- Coverage of key concepts from the source.<br>- Accuracy and faithfulness.<br>- No misleading omissions.<br>- No incorrect or irrelevant additions.<br>- Whether the content helps the audience understand the topic.<br><br>Low scores: missing ideas, distortions, fluff, unreliable<br><br>High scores: complete, accurate, relevant, useful |
| Storytelling | How well the deck works as a cohesive narrative.<br><br>Look for:<br>- Clear structure and progression.<br>- Strong transitions.<br>- Logical or emotional build.<br>- Clear purpose and momentum.<br>- Feels engaging, not list-like.<br><br>Low scores: disconnected, repetitive, flat<br><br>High scores: clear flow, intentional structure |
| Aesthetics | How polished and visually appealing the deck feels.<br><br>Look for:<br>- Effective color use and harmony.<br>- Consistency across slides.<br>- Enough visual variety.<br>- Quality of charts, diagrams, images, icons.<br><br>Low scores: sloppy, inconsistent, dated, harsh, generic<br><br>High scores: polished, professional, coherent |
| Layout | How elements are arranged within and across slides.<br><br>Look for:<br>- Clear spacing and margins.<br>- Consistent alignment.<br>- Logical grouping.<br>- Strong visual hierarchy.<br>- No clutter or overlap.<br>- Easy to scan.<br><br>Low scores: crowded, misaligned, confusing<br><br>High scores: clean, structured, readable |
| Editability | How easy the deck is to update and reuse.<br><br>Look for:<br>- Movable, editable elements.<br>- Avoidance of full slide images.<br>- Maintainable charts, text, and shapes.<br><br>Low scores: hard to modify, flattened, fragile<br><br>High scores: flexible, reusable, easy to update |
| Overall | Your holistic judgment of the deck as a complete presentation artifact. Use the criteria above as key inputs, but do not treat them as a full checklist. Consider any additional factors that affect real-world quality, usefulness, and readiness. Ask yourself:<br>- How good is this deck overall?<br>- How ready is it for real use?<br>- How much revision is needed before it can be shared or presented confidently?<br><br>Important: Overall is not a simple average of the scores. A major weakness in one area in a deck can significantly lower the score, even if other areas are strong. |

# Rating Rubric

- Use the full scale. Do not cluster scores in the middle.

| Rating | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| 1 - Very Poor | Severely broken or unusable.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Content is wrong, missing, or misleading<br>- No clear story<br>- Chaotic or unreadable design<br>- Layout blocks understanding<br>- Requires near-total rebuild |
| 2 - Poor | Major weaknesses make the deck risky to use.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Significant content gaps or accuracy issues<br>- Confusing structure<br>- Clear visual or layout failures |
| 3 - Fair | Below a solid standard.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Some useful content, but key gaps remain<br>- Storytelling, design or layout issues<br>- Needs meaningful revision |
| 4 - Mixed | Adequate baseline with clear strengths and weaknesses.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Core ideas are mostly present<br>- Deck basically works<br>- Noticeable rough edges<br>- Quality is uneven<br>- Suitable as an internal draft, not polished |
| 5 - Good | Strong, usable deck with some fixable issues.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Solid, mostly complete content<br>- Clear narrative flow<br>- Professional visuals<br>- Clean layout<br>- Needs moderate polish, not major changes |
| 6 - Very good | High-quality deck that is close to presentation ready.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Strong across nearly all criteria<br>- Clear and engaging<br>- Only minor weaknesses or polish issues remain |
| 7 - Excellent | Outstanding deck that fully satisfies the task.<br><br>Typical signs:<br>- Highly polished, readable, and visually consistent<br>- Only minor issues<br>- Feels professional and credible |
- Ready for real use with light polish

- Outstanding with no meaningful weaknesses. This should be used sparingly.

  We donʼt want a laundry list of issues , and youʼre not expected to catch everything. Focus on the key issues and explain why they matter .

- I have not already completed this task.

- I read the task prompt.

- I downloaded all responses and fully reviewed each one.

- I reviewed any provided sources.

- I validated factual claims via web search where applicable.

- I used the Grading Criteria if relevant and Rating Rubric in Rating Responses section when working on the task.

- I rated each response independently and gave an overall rating.

- Iʼve checked all responses for spelling, grammar and formatting errors and ensured each rationale is clearly and concisely written.

# Fit and Confidence

- I read the user request before claiming to check fit.

- The task sits within my domain expertise or clear comfort area.

- I'm confident in my ratings; if not, I did not submit.

# Individual Rating

- I rated all decks provided scale.
- I focused on overall deck quality , not isolated slide moments.
- I wrote a clear and concise rationale for each, ideally with examples.
- I rated each deck as independently as possible – one deck's weakness didn't inflate the other's score.

# Preferred Deck

- I selected a preferred deck.
- I compared overall deck quality, not slide-by-slide.
- My rationale for the preference is informative and specific, not just "A is better." If there was a tie-break, I used my best judgement and provided a clear rationale.
