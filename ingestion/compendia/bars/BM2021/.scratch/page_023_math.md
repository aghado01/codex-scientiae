To   conclude,   as   also   shown   in   the   simulation   study,   this   example   illustrates   the   fact   that   the   least   squares   approach   leads   to   estimators   seriously   affected   by   atypical   observations.   In   contrast,   the   robust   procedure   gives   more   reliable   results   automatically   down–weighting   potential   outliers.   The   robust   ﬁt   also   allows   to   identify   potential   atypical   observations   and   leads   to   estimated   additive   components     η j that   are   almost   identical   to   those   obtained   with   the   classical   procedure   applied   to   the   data   set   without   the   detected   outliers.

In   this   paper,   we   presented   a   procedure   to   robustly   estimate   the   regression   parameter   and   the   additive   components   under   a   partially   linear   additive   model.   The   method   combines   B -splines   to   smoothly   estimate   the   additive   components   and   MM -estimators.   The   use   of   B -splines   avoids   the   higher   complexity   of   kernels   methods   which   require   backﬁtting   or   marginal   integration   combined   with   a   proﬁle   approach,   see,   for   instance,   Li   ( 2000 ) or   Ma   and   Yang   ( 2011 ) for   a   discussion   on   the   computational   problems   arising   in   partially   linear   additive   model   when   using   kernel   methods.   In   contrast   to   Ma   and   Yang   ( 2011 ),   we   do   not   consider   an   additional   step   in   the   procedure   using   kernel   smoothers   to   obtain   estimators   of   the   nonparametric   components   for   which   the   limiting   distribution   can   be   derived.   However,   our   proposal   can   be   extended   to   provide   a   robust   spline-backﬁtted   kernel   smoother   combining   the   ideas   in   Ma   and   Yang   ( 2011 ) to   construct   pseudo– observations   with   the   robust   kernel   smoothers   deﬁned   in   Boente   and   Fraiman   ( 1989 ).   This   important   issue   is   beyond   the   scope   of   the   paper   and   will   be   object   of   future   work.

For   the   robust   B -spline   MM -estimators   proposed   consistency   results   and   rates   of   convergence   are   obtained.   Besides,   the   asymptotic   distribution   of   the   linear   regression   estimator   is   derived   under   mild   conditions.   The   numerical   results   illustrate   the   stability   of   the   proposed   methods   under   the   considered   contaminations   and   models.   We   exemplify   our   proposal   on   the   well   known   Boston   housing   data   set.   The   analysis   shows   that   the   robust   MM -estimators   automatically   discard   inﬂuential   observations   leading   to   reliable   estimates.

An   important   topic   to   be   discussed   is   the   feasibility   of   the   procedure   when   the   number   of   additive   components   is   large.   Even   though,   in   linear   regression   models,   Smucler   ( 2019 ) derived   consistency   results   for   S and   MM -regression   estimators   when   the   ratio   between   the   number   of   covariates   and   the   sample   size   converges   to   0,   it   is   well   known   that   the   number   of   subsamples   needed   to   obtain   good   candidates   for   the   S -estimator   increases   rapidly   with   the   number   of   covariates   and   that   the   estimator   becomes   unreliable   if   only   a   small   number   of   candidates   is   used,   see   Section   5.7.4   in   Maronna   et   al.   ( 2019 ).   Therefore,   under   a   partial   additive   linear   model,   when   p is   large   and   taking   into   account   that   the   total   number   of   parameters   may   be   even   larger   due   to   the   basis   dimension,   the   computation   of   the   scale   estimator   which   uses   an   S estimator   may   be   impractical.   As   described   in   Maronna   et   al.   ( 2019 ),   an   alternative   is   to   use   the   outlier   detection   method   introduced   in   Peña   and   Yohai   ( 1999 ) to   compute   the   initial   scale   estimator.   For   large   values   of   p and   q,   some   penalization   as   the   Ridge,   LASSO   or   SCAD   penalty   will   be   needed   in   order   to   make   the   estimators   computable.   One   possibility   is   to   penalize   the   objective   function   in   ( 6 ) by   adding   a   penalization   on   the   regression   parameters   if   β is   assumed   to   be   sparse   and   a   group   LASSO   method   for   selecting   the   coeﬃcients   of   the   spline   approximations,   as   done   in   Huang   et   al.   ( 2010 ) for   additive   models.   The   study   of   such   robust   penalized   estimators   is   an   interesting   and   challenging   topic,   that   will   be   object   of   future   work.
