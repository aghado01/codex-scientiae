$$
q(M_{k+1} \mid M_k) = b_k\,\frac{1}{k}\sum_i h_B(\xi_{\mathrm{cand}} \mid \xi_i);
$$

in the expression $q(M_{k+1} \mid M_k)$ there is a mixture of densities because the new knot $\xi_{\mathrm{cand}}$ can be generated by any of $k$ different distributions.

Death step. The candidate knot for deletion is chosen uniformly from the set of existing knots. Let $M_k$ be the current model. Then the jump probability of going from $M_k$ to $M_{k-1}$ is

$$
q(M_{k-1} \mid M_k) = d_k\,\frac{1}{k}.
$$

Relocation step. We first choose a candidate knot $\xi_{j^*}$ uniformly from the set of existing knots $\{\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_k\}$. The candidate new location, $\xi_{\mathrm{cand}}$, for the knot $\xi_{j^*}$ is generated by a distribution centred at $\xi_{j^*}$ with spread parameter $\tau_R$ and having density $h_R(\xi_c \mid \xi_{j^*}, \tau_R)$.

Let $\xi = (\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_{j^*-1}, \xi_{j^*}, \xi_{j^*+1}, \ldots, \xi_k)$ be the current sequence of knots, and let $\xi^c = (\xi_1, \ldots, \xi_{j^*-1}, \xi_{\mathrm{cand}}, \xi_{j^*+1}, \ldots, \xi_k)^T$ be the candidate new sequence of knots, which differs from $\xi$ only in the replacement of knot $\xi_{j^*}$ by knot $\xi_{\mathrm{cand}}$. Note that the candidate new location does not have to be the $j^*$th element. The jump probability is computed as follows:

$$
q(M_{\mathrm{cand}} \mid M_{\mathrm{curr}}) = \eta_k\,\frac{1}{k}\,h_R(\xi_{\mathrm{cand}} \mid \xi_{j^*}).
$$

Candidate distributions. One convenient choice for the birth and relocation proposal distributions, with densities $h_B$ and $h_R$, would be Beta distributions. The precise form, however, is not likely to make much difference. Once these are selected, it remains to choose values of parameters $c$, $\tau_B$ and $\tau_R$. In principle, these may be regarded as tuning parameters, adjusted to produce a chain having good acceptance probabilities.

Here, we choose the constant $c$ to be $0.4$ as in Denison et al. (1998); a limited study of our own suggests that this is a good value. We take both the birth and relocation proposals to be Beta distributions with parameters $\xi_{j^*} n$ and $(1 - \xi_{j^*}) n$, and we choose $n = 50$ in our examples. We obtained essentially the same results using for the birth and relocation densities $h_B$ and $h_R$ a Normal distribution with mean $\xi_{j^*}$ and variance $\tau^2$, truncated to $[\xi_{j^*} - 2, \xi_{j^*} + 2]$.
