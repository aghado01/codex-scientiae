I read through the repo — README, the doccer planning canon (roadmap/decisions/status registry), `src/doccer/README.md`, and the file layout. Short version: no, they don't make it moot, and the comparison is less flattering to LILO/Leroy than you'd expect.

## What you actually built vs. what they built

Doccer is a many-sorted algebra with frozen contracts: `P/L/I/C/F/O/B/U` carriers, an identity floor where spans bound to one master refuse to validate against another, Allen relations with a JEPD-closed 13-bit `AllenRelationSet`, path selection with decision residue, laminar/packing validation, `FactSaturation` as a finite positive least closure, `OriginRelation` for output-to-source lineage, and `RewritePlan`/`Materialize` producing gap-free reconstructing piece partitions. 62 C# files, 2751 checks, 47 numbered decisions, Lean gated by explicit burden triggers.

Library learning does none of that. It takes a corpus of already-written programs, finds repeated subtrees, and factors them out to compress the corpus. That's it. The objective function is compression. There is no type discipline beyond the DSL's own, no identity semantics, no evidence, no residue, no notion of refusing to collapse. [Leroy's](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06438v1) headline result is 6 valid abstractions found in a subset of Python (no lambdas, no nested defs, no classes) at a 20-AST-node threshold, one of which assigns two params and prints their sum, used at three call sites. [LILO](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.19791v4) is much stronger work but its domains are REGEX, CLEVR, and LOGO turtle graphics, with synthetic templated language descriptions. Neither is operating anywhere near your problem's complexity.

## Where they genuinely bear on it

This is the part worth your attention. Your "abductive census" is manual library learning, and the roadmap says so almost in their vocabulary: the engine's latent path-space, verbs that name latent call paths, a capability surface carved from that space by witnessed demand, catalog-before-naming, composition-before-extension with a failed-composition argument as proof obligation for any mechanism claim.

Substitute terms: latent path-space = the space of DSL compositions. Verb = learned abstraction. "Composition before extension" = exactly Stitch's test of whether a candidate is expressible in the existing primitives. "Witnessed demand" = the reuse-frequency term in the compression objective. LILO's AutoDoc — which takes an anonymous learned function plus its type signature, expression, existing library docs, and usage examples, and emits a name and docstring — is a mechanized version of your promotion step.

So the relationship isn't "they subsume you," it's that they offer a **mechanical adversary for your census**. You have a corpus sitting right there: 129 `.ps1` files of provisional adapters reaching into the packaged DLL, plus the graveyard repair farm (`repair_*` ×~30) that the roadmap already designates an evidence pool for F1 edit plans. Leroy's contribution is precisely the recipe for feeding imperative code to Stitch — lispify the AST, nested `StatementList` for statement sequences — and `stitch_core` is a pip-installable Rust binary, unlike DreamCoder. Run it over lispified call sequences against the Doccer surface and you get a compression-ranked list of candidate abstractions. Every candidate it proposes that your census never named is a census miss to explain; every verb you named that it also finds is independent confirmation that demand was real and not aesthetic. That's cheap, it's falsifiable, and it attacks the one part of your process that currently has no oracle — human abduction deciding what deserves a name.

The sharper application: `RewritePlan` is already an exact-source-basis **ordered output program** with closed piece modes. That's a corpus of programs in a DSL with exact origin semantics. Library learning over accumulated rewrite plans would give you learned materialization macros with provenance intact — which is a real research direction nobody has done, because nobody else has the origin carrier.

## Where you should not import them

Compression-optimal abstractions are not contract-legitimate ones. Stitch will cheerfully anti-unify across your sorts, which is the exact confusion your identity floor exists to make fail loudly — diagonal empties into Allen `I`, geometric `Equal` standing in for occurrence identity, compatible masters substituting for stage identity. Treat its output as census specimens, never as promotions. Its ranking is a prior over what to examine, and your D-canon remains the adjudicator. Also worth noting that [babble's](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04596) library-learning-modulo-theory is e-graphs plus equality saturation — the same machinery family as your K5b least-closure engine, pointed at a different problem. If you want to read one paper for transferable technique rather than for comparison, that's the one.

Your project isn't simplistic by comparison; it's solving a harder problem (exact, contract-frozen, evidence-bearing document transformation) and deliberately doing by hand the one step they automate.
