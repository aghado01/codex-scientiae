Your instinct is right, and it's worth saying precisely _where_ it's right: almost every sort in the deep-dive has a named formal home once you know which community to look in. The finite, discrete setting isn't a gap in the literature — it's scattered across four or five literatures that don't cite each other. A map, by kernel sort:

**The whole kernel, nearly verbatim: document spanners.** Fagin, Kimelfeld, Reiss, Vansummeren, "Document Spanners: A Formal Approach to Information Extraction" (PODS 2013 / JACM 2015). A spanner maps a finite string to a _relation over spans_ — and their spans are half-open `[i,j⟩` with empty spans at positions, literally your TextSpan including empties-as-positions. The decade of follow-on work is your Tranche B and beyond, already formalized: closure properties of the span-relational algebra (core spanners are famously _not_ closed under difference — a formal warning for §8's `Subtract`), positive Datalog over span predicates (the "recursive spanners"/RGXlog line — Peterfreund, ten Cate, Kimelfeld, Vansummeren, ICDT 2019 — which is §6.2 exactly), constant-delay enumeration of results, and "Split-Correctness in Information Extraction" (Doleschal, Kimelfeld, Martens et al., PODS 2019) — which formalizes _when running an extractor per-chunk and unioning equals running it on the whole document_: the chunker factory's correctness question, as a theorem schema. The industrial ancestor is SystemT/AQL (Chiticariu, Reiss et al.), whose "consolidate" policies are your suppression, as store data.

**The finite-master Allen caveat has a name: weak representation.** Ligozat's framework (the Ligozat & Renz paper Sol cites, expanded in Ligozat's 2011 book) defines representations where composition is only an upper bound. The precise sentence you're entitled to: _a finite discrete master is a weak representation of Allen's interval algebra that is not a representation._ That's §3.2 in five words of standard terminology, with Hirsch & Hodkinson's _Relation Algebras by Games_ behind it if you ever want the representability theory proper.

**The located-language algebra is the incidence algebra of a chain.** Sets of pairs `(i,j), i≤j` with union and concatenation-through-shared-boundary = the Boolean-semiring incidence algebra (Rota 1964) = **upper-triangular Boolean matrices** with entrywise-or and matrix product. That identification buys every law for free: square matrices over a Kleene algebra form a Kleene algebra (Kozen 1994; Conway 1971), and nonempty tokens are _strictly_ upper triangular, hence nilpotent, hence `A*` is a finite sum — your "Many must consume" caveat becomes a two-line proposition rather than a policy. Valiant (1975) then connects §6.2 to the same carrier: chart parsing _is_ transitive closure of upper-triangular matrices. On the practice side, this object is the word lattice of speech recognition, with Mohri's weighted-automata algorithms as the path-fold library. And Moszkowski's Interval Temporal Logic — over finite discrete state sequences from the start — has `Seq` as its _chop_ operator and `Many` as chop-star; Halpern–Shoham over finite linear orders (the Montanari school) is the modal-logic face of the Allen sort.

**Pairing: nested words.** Alur & Madhusudan's nested words / visibly pushdown languages (JACM 2009) add an explicit _matching relation_ to a finite word, with axioms (properly nested, non-crossing) — precisely because bracket-matching is not recoverable from flat geometry by regular means. That is the composition-refusal argument, and the containment-≠-parenthood point, as established theory. The pending pairing mint has a canonical formal citation waiting for it.

**Facts vs. derivations: provenance semirings.** Green, Karvounarakis, Tannen (PODS 2007): annotated relations where a fact carries a provenance polynomial whose monomials are its derivations — your canonical-fact-once, many-supports rule, as the universal construction. Why-provenance (Buneman, Khanna, Tan) is witness-retention; stratified Datalog (Apt–Blair–Walker) is the staged-negation discipline §8 gestures at. This also covers claim identity: SpanBatch is a K-relation (bag-annotated) over the span domain, which is why equal geometry with distinct rows is licensed rather than awkward.

**Selection and multiresolution.** Knuth–Plass line breaking (1981) is the budgeted boundary-DAG shortest path with penalties and protected breaks — the RAG-chunking cost structure, forty years early. Weighted interval scheduling and changepoint DP (PELT) cover packing and partition variants; Reps gives maximal munch a formal linear-time treatment (TOPLAS 1998). Two things are _derivable_ rather than findable, each a one-paragraph proof: interval partitions of a window correspond to subsets of internal boundaries, so the refinement order on ResolutionViews is literally the Boolean lattice of cut-points; and the crossing graph of spans is a circle graph (overlap graphs of intervals = circle graphs), where maximum independent set is polynomial (Gavril 1973) — meaning an _optimal_ Laminarizer is classical, and the current greedy has a named upgrade path.

**Morphisms.** Monotone maps between finite ordered carriers are the simplicial category Δ: every OffsetMap factors as collapse-then-embed (epi-mono factorization), TextSlice is a mono. For spans surviving edits, the CRDT/OT literature on stable position identifiers is the formal cousin of the staleness-guard problem.

The honest residual — what I _can't_ point at: multi-source, relation-valued origins composed across a chain of materialized masters. Bojańczyk's origins are functional (one input position per output position); marrying that with K-relation provenance is straightforward but doesn't seem to have a name. That, plus the many-sorted assembly itself, is the original artifact — which is exactly where §12's honesty table already put it. And that's the strong position, not the weak one: no novel mathematics means every tranche can cite its laws, and half these pointers convert directly into oracle-diff tests the way D6 already does.
