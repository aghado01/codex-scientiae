## 1. The `jobs.ps1` argument is factually wrong

The plan's whole case for hoisting jobs to `shared/` is: *"making it take `-Bootstrap` and `-Work` explicitly is what removes the re-dot-source, since the whole reason for that dot-source was to reconstitute ambient state."*

It isn't. The worker at [arxiv.ps1:697](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:697) does `. $WLibPath` and *then* `Use-ArxivRateState $WRateState`. The dot-source is there because a runspace built from `InitialSessionState::CreateDefault()` has no access to the parent's function table — the worker needs `Invoke-ArxivFetch` and its whole transitive closure to exist as *code*. Parameterizing the clock deletes one line; the dot-source stays, it just moves into the caller-supplied `-Bootstrap`.

The conclusion survives (a queue+worker that doesn't know about `arxiv.ps1` is the right primitive, and post-split it dot-sources a much smaller surface). The *pay-immediately* argument doesn't. By the plan's own stated test, that means jobs is a one-witness generalization — put it in `shared/` if you want, but on the honest grounds, not this one.

Parameterization does earn its keep elsewhere, though: [`Initialize-ArxivJobs`](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:678)'s `if ($script:ArxivJobs) { return }` silently discards a second call's staging root. Ambient state is what makes that possible.

## The broken updates are real, and there are four

**Zenodo destroys prior artifact records on every re-fetch.** [zenodo.ps1:260](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/zenodo.ps1:260) builds the sidecar fresh with `artifacts = $results` and never reads the existing file. arXiv has exactly this guard at [arxiv.ps1:568](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:568) with a comment explaining why. So `-Artifacts pdf` then `-Artifacts source` leaves a sidecar claiming only `source` exists, while the PDF sits on disk unrecorded.

**arXiv downgrades the record it touches.** The already-present path writes a four-field record `{staged, already_present, path, bytes}` at [arxiv.ps1:502](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:502); the merge then does `$artifactRecords[$k] = $results[$k]` unconditionally at [:576](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:576). Re-fetching `pdf,source` when the PDF is already staged strips `url`, `kind`, and `fetched_at` from the PDF record. The merge preserves *untouched* artifacts but not *unchanged* ones — lossy in precisely the case it was written to protect.

**Sci-Hub acquisitions are invisible to the inbox.** `Invoke-ScihubFetch` writes `{slug}.scihub.json` with a completely different shape — no `artifacts` map, no `id`/`idv` ([scihub-get.ps1:126](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/scihub-get.ps1:126)) — into the *same* staging root that scholar's `acquire` passes it ([scholar-server.ps1:190](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/mcp-servers/procurement/scholar-server.ps1:190)). `Get-ArxivInbox` discovers by the `.arxiv.json` suffix ([arxiv.ps1:606](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:606)), so every DOI-routed paper is staged and then unlistable. Three writers, three schemas, one directory, and the survey knows one of them.

**And a malformed sidecar silently deletes a paper from the listing** — `catch { continue }` at [arxiv.ps1:610](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/arxiv.ps1:610). A torn write doesn't error; the paper just stops existing.

Plus two pieces of state with no store at all: `$script:ScihubLastGood` ([scihub-get.ps1:87](D:/aghado01/graveyard/codex-scientiae/src/procurement/scihub-get.ps1:87)) is the only *learned* state in the system and dies with the process, and the job registry is a ConcurrentDictionary evicted after ~30 minutes — restart the server and every completed fetch result is gone.

## What the engine can take over

Three tiers, and it already covers two:

- **Per-item sidecars** → `write_json`/`read_json`, which replaces four hand-rolled `ConvertTo-Json | WriteAllText(UTF8Encoding($false))` sites and puts them on the declared encoding/Codec axes. But note this alone does *not* fix the bugs — the engine gives you format, and read-modify-write stays the caller's problem, which is exactly where both defects live.
- **The inbox itself as a `Registry` store.** [kinds/registry.py](src/shared/jsonl_engine/kinds/registry.py) already has `key_of`, `collate`, `rebuild`, and `DuplicateEntry`. Keyed on `(provider, id)`, one row per staged item, this is the actual fix: the directory-walk-and-parse-everything survey disappears, Sci-Hub items are visible by construction, the silent-skip becomes a validation failure, and "re-fetching one artifact preserves the others" becomes a *collate* rule in one place instead of two hand-written merges that disagree.
- **Job records** → append-only JSONL is their natural shape, and the lease/commit discipline gives you the concurrency story the ConcurrentDictionary is standing in for. Survives restart for free, which is the thing a human scripting outside MCP most needs.

## The gating gap

The engine's shell surface is **read-only**. `cli.py`'s verbs are info/count/verify/sig/head/tail/range/get/select/find/snapshot/schemas/json; `jso-shell.ps1` exports `Get-Jsonl*`, `Select-JsonlPath`, `Find-JsonlRecord`, `Test-JsonlStore`, `New-JsonlSnapshot`, `Read-JsonDocument`. `JsonlEngine.append`/`commit` and `write_json` exist in Python with no CLI verb and no PowerShell function in front of them. So today the PS lane can read through the engine and cannot write — none of the above is reachable until that changes.

That's your small addition, but it forks into a real choice worth making deliberately: shell-out-per-write is fine for a sidecar and bad for a job log (one process per record); or procurement's state layer moves to Python and PS calls it; or PS keeps writing bytes and the engine owns only the *format contract*, which the byte-equality gate and golden fixtures actually make testable.

Worth noting that under your class direction this collapses nicely — if the state layer is `[InboxRegistry]` and `[JobLog]` with methods, which of those three answers you picked becomes an implementation detail behind one type, and you can start with the shell-out and change your mind later without touching a caller.
